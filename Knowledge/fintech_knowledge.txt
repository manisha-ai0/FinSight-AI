1. DIGITAL PAYMENTS — BASIC CONCEPT
============================================================

A digital payment is a payment in which money is transferred
electronically rather than through physical cash.

Examples include:

- UPI payments
- Bank account transfers
- Card payments
- Internet banking payments
- Mobile banking payments
- Wallet or prepaid payment instrument transactions
- Electronic bill payments
- Certain digital recurring-payment mechanisms

A digital payment normally involves multiple stages.

A simplified lifecycle is:

1. Payment initiation
2. Identification of the payer and payee
3. Transaction validation
4. Authentication and authorization
5. Transaction routing and processing
6. Clearing
7. Settlement
8. Confirmation
9. Reconciliation
10. Exception or dispute handling when required

Not every payment system implements these stages in exactly the
same technical manner.

============================================================
2. UPI — UNIFIED PAYMENTS INTERFACE
============================================================

UPI is an electronic payment system that enables users to make
bank-account-based payments using participating UPI applications
and banking infrastructure.

UPI can support payment experiences such as:

- Person-to-person payments
- Person-to-merchant payments
- QR-based payments
- UPI ID / Virtual Payment Address based payments
- Pay requests
- Collect requests
- Intent-based payments
- Other UPI-supported transaction mechanisms

UPI is designed to enable convenient account-to-account payments
without requiring users to manually enter full bank-account
details for every transaction.

A UPI ID is commonly represented in a form such as:

name@bank-or-provider

The exact format and availability depend on the participating
provider and UPI ecosystem.

============================================================
3. MAIN PARTICIPANTS IN THE UPI ECOSYSTEM
============================================================

A UPI transaction involves multiple participants and systems.

------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 NPCI
------------------------------------------------------------

NPCI stands for National Payments Corporation of India.

For UPI, NPCI functions as the network owner, network operator,
service provider and coordinator of the UPI network.

NPCI provides the infrastructure and ecosystem-level mechanisms
needed for participating members to communicate and process UPI
transactions.

NPCI also performs clearing and settlement-related functions for
UPI transactions between participating members according to the
UPI settlement mechanism.

The bot should distinguish between:

- NPCI as the UPI network infrastructure/operator
- Banks as account-holding and transaction-participating entities
- PSPs as payment service providers
- UPI applications as the user-facing interface

------------------------------------------------------------
3.2 Remitter Bank
------------------------------------------------------------

The remitter bank is the bank associated with the payer's account.

During a UPI transaction, the payer's bank account may be debited
for the transaction.

The remitter bank also has responsibilities related to
authentication and transaction processing.

For example, the customer's UPI PIN is authenticated through the
banking/payment infrastructure rather than by the chatbot or by
the merchant.

------------------------------------------------------------
3.3 Beneficiary Bank
------------------------------------------------------------

The beneficiary bank is the bank associated with the recipient's
account.

When a successful transaction results in money being credited to
the recipient, the beneficiary bank is involved in receiving and
crediting the funds.

------------------------------------------------------------
3.4 Payer PSP
------------------------------------------------------------

A Payer PSP is a payment service provider function associated with
the payer side.

A Payer PSP can onboard customers into UPI services and provide
the interface through which customers initiate or approve
transactions.

The payer-side experience may include:

- Customer registration
- UPI ID creation
- Device binding
- Transaction initiation
- Authentication interaction
- Transaction approval

------------------------------------------------------------
3.5 Payee PSP
------------------------------------------------------------

A Payee PSP is a payment service provider function associated
with the beneficiary or receiving side.

It can support onboarding of customers or merchants and receiving
money through UPI.

A Payee PSP may also support collect requests.

------------------------------------------------------------
3.6 Customer / Account Holder
------------------------------------------------------------

The customer is the person using the UPI-enabled bank account.

A customer generally needs:

- A UPI-enabled bank account
- A supported UPI application
- A registered mobile number as required by the ecosystem
- Appropriate authentication credentials
- A UPI ID or other supported payment identifier

============================================================
4. UPI PAYMENT IDENTIFIERS
============================================================

UPI supports several ways to identify the recipient depending on
the transaction flow.

Examples include:

- UPI ID / Virtual Payment Address
- QR code
- Mobile number where supported
- Bank account number and IFSC in supported flows
- Other identifiers supported by the UPI ecosystem

A UPI ID is intended to provide a convenient payment identifier
without requiring the payer to manually provide complete banking
information for every payment.

============================================================
5. BASIC UPI PAYMENT FLOW
============================================================

A simplified UPI payment can be understood as follows:

STEP 1 — PAYMENT INITIATION

The customer opens a UPI-enabled application and chooses to make
a payment.

The customer may:

- Enter a UPI ID
- Scan a QR code
- Select a contact
- Use an online merchant's UPI payment option
- Use another supported payment method

------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 2 — PAYMENT DETAILS

The payer specifies or confirms:

- Recipient
- Amount
- Payment purpose or description when applicable
- Other transaction information required by the application

The user should verify the recipient and amount before approving
the transaction.

------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 3 — TRANSACTION VALIDATION

The payment system validates the transaction information.

Examples of validation may include:

- Whether the destination is valid
- Whether required information is present
- Whether the account/payment address can be resolved
- Whether the transaction meets applicable rules
- Whether technical systems are available

A validation failure can result in a declined or failed
transaction.

------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 4 — AUTHENTICATION

The customer may be required to authenticate the transaction.

For conventional UPI bank-account payments, the UPI PIN is an
important transaction authentication mechanism.

Authentication should be distinguished from authorization.

Authentication asks:

"Can the system verify the user or required transaction
credential?"

Authorization asks:

"Should this transaction be permitted?"

------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 5 — AUTHORIZATION / PROCESSING

Once the required checks and authentication succeed, the
transaction can proceed through the relevant payment
infrastructure.

The transaction may pass through multiple participating entities
before completion.

------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 6 — DEBIT AND CREDIT

The payer-side account is debited and the beneficiary-side account
is credited as applicable to the transaction.

The actual movement and settlement of funds involves the
participating banks and the payment system infrastructure.

------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 7 — SETTLEMENT

Settlement is the process through which participating financial
institutions settle their obligations arising from payment
transactions.

For UPI, NPCI performs clearing and settlement-related functions
between participating members according to the applicable
settlement mechanism.

------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 8 — CONFIRMATION

The application and/or bank may provide a transaction status.

Possible outcomes include:

- Successful
- Failed
- Declined
- Pending
- Reversed
- Other system-specific statuses

The exact status terminology can vary.

------------------------------------------------------------

STEP 9 — RECONCILIATION

Participating institutions reconcile transaction records.

Reconciliation involves comparing transaction records between
systems to identify:

- Missing transactions
- Duplicate transactions
- Amount mismatches
- Incorrect statuses
- Failed transactions
- Reversed transactions
- Other exceptions

============================================================
6. PAYMENT INITIATION
============================================================

Payment initiation is the stage where the payer begins a payment.

Examples:

- Selecting "Pay"
- Scanning a QR code
- Entering a UPI ID
- Selecting a recipient
- Selecting UPI at an online checkout
- Approving a collect request

Payment initiation does NOT automatically mean the transaction
has completed.

A user pressing "Pay" is only the beginning of the transaction
lifecycle.

============================================================
7. PAYMENT VALIDATION
============================================================

Validation checks whether a transaction request satisfies the
technical and business requirements necessary for processing.

Possible validation areas include:

- Recipient identifier
- Account status
- Transaction format
- Amount
- Transaction limits
- Authentication state
- Availability of required systems
- Applicable transaction rules
- Risk and fraud controls

Validation and authentication are different.

Example:

Validation:
"Is the UPI ID in a usable format and can the transaction be
processed?"

Authentication:
"Has the required user authentication succeeded?"

============================================================
8. AUTHENTICATION
============================================================

Authentication is the process of verifying an identity or
credential.

In digital payments, authentication helps establish that the
person or device attempting the transaction satisfies the
required authentication mechanism.

Examples of authentication factors can include:

- PIN
- OTP
- Device binding
- Biometrics
- Other approved authentication mechanisms

The exact authentication method depends on the payment system,
transaction type, applicable regulations and implementation.

The RBI has emphasized authentication and security controls for
digital payments, including additional factors of authentication
and evolving authentication mechanisms.

IMPORTANT:

A chatbot must never ask a user to provide:

- UPI PIN
- ATM PIN
- Internet banking password
- OTP
- Card CVV
- Authentication credentials
- Full sensitive banking credentials

The chatbot must never store or process such credentials.

============================================================
9. AUTHORIZATION
============================================================

Authorization is the decision to permit or reject a transaction
after required checks have been performed.

Authorization may consider:

- Successful authentication
- Account status
- Available funds
- Transaction rules
- Risk controls
- Applicable limits
- Fraud signals
- System availability
- Compliance requirements

Authorization does not mean that the chatbot itself is
authorizing a real transaction.

The FinTech Explainer Bot is informational only.

============================================================
10. TRANSACTION PROCESSING
============================================================

Transaction processing refers to the technical and operational
steps required to move a transaction through the payment
infrastructure.

Processing can involve:

- Payment applications
- PSP systems
- Banks
- NPCI infrastructure
- Merchant systems
- Payment gateways or other intermediaries depending on the
  payment type

The exact path depends on the payment system and transaction
type.

============================================================
11. CLEARING
============================================================

Clearing is associated with determining the obligations arising
between participating institutions from payment transactions.

In a simplified model:

Transactions occur
        ↓
Transaction information is collected
        ↓
Obligations are calculated
        ↓
Participants determine what they owe or are owed
        ↓
Settlement occurs

Clearing and settlement are related but are not identical.

============================================================
12. SETTLEMENT
============================================================

Settlement is the process by which financial obligations between
participating institutions are finally discharged.

For UPI, NPCI acts as a clearing and settlement agency for
inter-bank transactions between participating remitter and
beneficiary members.

The UPI settlement process includes calculation of net debit and
credit positions for participating members.

A simplified representation is:

UPI transactions
        ↓
NPCI network processing
        ↓
Net obligations between members
        ↓
Settlement instructions
        ↓
Member settlement accounts
        ↓
Final settlement

The settlement process is different from the user-facing payment
confirmation.

A customer may see a successful payment message while the
underlying inter-bank settlement processes occur through the
payment system infrastructure.

============================================================
13. RECONCILIATION
============================================================

Reconciliation means comparing records from different systems to
ensure that they agree.

For example:

Bank transaction records
        VS
Payment network records

If the records do not match, an exception may need to be
investigated.

Common reconciliation problems include:

- Transaction appears in one system but not another
- Duplicate transaction
- Wrong amount
- Wrong status
- Missing credit
- Missing debit
- Delayed response
- Reversal
- Technical failure

Reconciliation is important because payment systems consist of
multiple interconnected systems.

============================================================
14. PAYMENT REQUEST TYPES
============================================================

UPI can support different transaction patterns.

------------------------------------------------------------
14.1 Pay / Push Transaction
------------------------------------------------------------

In a push-style payment, the payer actively initiates the
transfer of funds to the beneficiary.

Example:

Customer selects a merchant
        ↓
Customer enters/scans payment details
        ↓
Customer enters amount
        ↓
Customer authenticates
        ↓
Payment is processed

------------------------------------------------------------
14.2 Collect / Pull Request
------------------------------------------------------------

In a collect request, a payment request is sent to a payer.

The payer reviews the request and can approve or reject it.

A collect request should not be interpreted as automatic
permission to debit an account.

The payer must follow the applicable authentication and approval
process.

------------------------------------------------------------
14.3 Intent-Based Payment
------------------------------------------------------------

Intent-based UPI payments can allow a user to select a UPI
application during online checkout.

The merchant checkout provides payment information to the
selected UPI application.

The user then reviews and authenticates the transaction.

============================================================
15. QR PAYMENTS
============================================================

A QR code can encode payment-related information.

In a typical QR payment:

1. Customer opens a UPI application.
2. Customer scans the merchant's QR code.
3. Payment information is displayed.
4. Customer checks the recipient and amount.
5. Customer authenticates.
6. Transaction is processed.
7. Transaction status is returned.

Security principle:

Scanning a QR code does not itself guarantee that the transaction
is safe.

Users should verify:

- Recipient name
- Amount
- Payment purpose
- Transaction request

A QR code can be used for both legitimate and fraudulent
purposes, so the user should verify payment details before
authorization.

============================================================
16. MERCHANT PAYMENTS
============================================================

A merchant payment involves a customer paying a business.

Simplified flow:

Customer
   ↓
Merchant checkout
   ↓
UPI payment option
   ↓
UPI application
   ↓
Customer authentication
   ↓
Payment network
   ↓
Banks
   ↓
Merchant receives transaction result

Merchant systems may also perform their own order and payment
status reconciliation.

A payment confirmation should not automatically be treated as
proof that a particular business order was fulfilled.

Business systems should reconcile payment status with order
records.

============================================================
17. UPI 24x7 AVAILABILITY
============================================================

NPCI's UPI FAQ describes UPI payments as available around the
clock.

However, availability of a specific transaction can still be
affected by:

- Bank downtime
- Network problems
- Application issues
- System maintenance
- Technical failures
- Risk controls
- Transaction restrictions

Therefore:

"UPI is designed for round-the-clock payments"

does not mean:

"Every transaction will always succeed."

============================================================
18. BUSINESS DECLINE VS TECHNICAL DECLINE
============================================================

A transaction can fail for different reasons.

------------------------------------------------------------
18.1 Business Decline
------------------------------------------------------------

A business decline is a decline caused by transaction/business
conditions rather than a technical failure.

Examples can include:

- Invalid authentication/PIN
- Incorrect beneficiary information
- Exceeding an applicable transaction limit
- Exceeding a permitted transaction count
- Other business-rule conditions

------------------------------------------------------------
18.2 Technical Decline
------------------------------------------------------------

A technical decline occurs because of technical problems.

Examples:

- System unavailability
- Network problems
- Bank-side technical failure
- Payment-system technical issues

This distinction is useful when explaining why a payment failed.

============================================================
19. PAYMENT STATUS
============================================================

A payment system can produce different transaction states.

Common conceptual states include:

SUCCESSFUL
The transaction has completed successfully according to the
system's final status.

FAILED
The transaction could not be completed.

DECLINED
The transaction was rejected.

PENDING
The final status has not yet been established.

REVERSED
A previously processed transaction has been reversed according
to the applicable transaction process.

IMPORTANT:

The exact meaning and handling of statuses depends on the
payment system and specific transaction flow.

The chatbot must not invent a status interpretation when the
actual system status is unknown.

============================================================
20. KYC — KNOW YOUR CUSTOMER
============================================================

KYC means Know Your Customer.

KYC is a regulatory framework and process used by regulated
entities to identify and understand their customers.

The RBI's KYC framework includes concepts such as:

- Customer Acceptance Policy
- Customer Identification Procedure
- Customer Due Diligence
- Beneficial ownership
- Ongoing due diligence
- Enhanced due diligence
- Simplified due diligence where applicable
- Risk management

KYC helps regulated entities establish who their customers are
and manage associated financial-crime risks.

============================================================
21. CUSTOMER IDENTIFICATION
============================================================

Customer identification involves establishing and verifying
customer identity according to applicable requirements.

Depending on the entity and applicable rules, this can involve:

- Identity information
- Address information
- Officially valid documents
- Customer due diligence
- Verification procedures
- Risk classification

The exact requirements depend on the applicable RBI directions,
laws and circumstances.

The chatbot should not tell a user:

"Submit this exact document and your account will definitely be
approved."

Instead it should explain that requirements depend on the
regulated entity and applicable KYC rules.

============================================================
22. CUSTOMER DUE DILIGENCE — CDD
============================================================

Customer Due Diligence is the process through which a regulated
entity obtains and evaluates relevant customer information.

CDD can include:

- Identifying the customer
- Verifying identity
- Understanding the nature and purpose of the relationship
- Understanding risk
- Identifying beneficial owners where applicable
- Monitoring transactions as required

CDD is broader than simply collecting an identity document.

============================================================
23. ONGOING DUE DILIGENCE
============================================================

Customer due diligence does not necessarily end when an account
is opened.

Regulated entities may need ongoing monitoring to ensure that
customer activity is consistent with the institution's
understanding of the customer and applicable risk requirements.

This can involve:

- Transaction monitoring
- Updating customer information
- Reviewing risk
- Investigating unusual activity
- Applying enhanced due diligence where appropriate

============================================================
24. BENEFICIAL OWNER
============================================================

A beneficial owner is the natural person who ultimately owns or
controls a legal entity or arrangement according to applicable
rules.

Identifying beneficial ownership is important because criminals
may attempt to hide the actual person controlling or benefiting
from financial activity behind companies or other structures.

The exact definition and thresholds are governed by applicable
laws and regulations.

============================================================
25. AML — ANTI-MONEY LAUNDERING
============================================================

Money laundering is the process of disguising proceeds from
illegal activities so that they appear legitimate.

AML refers to measures used to prevent, detect and respond to
money-laundering risks.

Common AML concepts include:

- Customer identification
- Customer due diligence
- Transaction monitoring
- Risk assessment
- Suspicious activity identification
- Record keeping
- Reporting obligations
- Internal controls

The chatbot should explain AML conceptually rather than provide
instructions for avoiding AML controls.

============================================================
26. CFT — COUNTERING FINANCING OF TERRORISM
============================================================

CFT refers to measures designed to prevent and detect financing
of terrorism.

AML and CFT are related financial-crime control frameworks.

They can involve:

- Customer due diligence
- Risk-based monitoring
- Screening
- Transaction monitoring
- Reporting
- Record keeping
- Controls against prohibited activity

The exact requirements depend on applicable law and regulation.

============================================================
27. SANCTIONS AND SCREENING
============================================================

Financial institutions and relevant regulated entities may have
screening and control obligations relating to prohibited persons,
entities or transactions.

Screening can be part of financial-crime compliance.

A screening alert does not automatically mean that a person has
committed a crime.

An alert can require investigation or review.

The chatbot must avoid making accusations about real people or
organizations based only on hypothetical screening results.

============================================================
28. TRANSACTION MONITORING
============================================================

Transaction monitoring involves observing transactions for
patterns or activity that may require review.

Examples of potentially unusual patterns can include:

- Activity inconsistent with a customer's expected profile
- Unusual transaction patterns
- Rapid movement of funds
- Repeated unusual transactions
- Structuring or suspicious transaction patterns
- Activity associated with known fraud indicators

Important:

An unusual transaction is not automatically illegal.

A monitoring alert generally indicates that additional review
may be necessary.

============================================================
29. RISK-BASED APPROACH
============================================================

Financial-crime compliance commonly uses a risk-based approach.

A risk-based approach means controls can be designed according to
the level and type of risk rather than treating every situation
as identical.

Possible risk factors can include:

- Customer type
- Product
- Geography
- Transaction pattern
- Delivery channel
- Business relationship
- Other relevant factors

Higher-risk situations can require stronger controls.

============================================================
30. DIGITAL PAYMENT SECURITY
============================================================

Digital payment security involves protecting:

- Customer accounts
- Payment applications
- Authentication credentials
- Transaction data
- Payment infrastructure
- Communication channels
- Backend systems
- APIs
- Databases
- Devices

RBI has issued digital-payment security controls for regulated
entities and separate cyber-resilience and digital-payment
security directions for non-bank payment system operators.

Security controls can cover areas such as:

- Governance
- Risk management
- Application security
- Authentication
- Fraud risk management
- Reconciliation
- Customer awareness
- Grievance redressal
- Incident management

============================================================
31. DEVICE SECURITY
============================================================

The user's device is part of the security chain.

Potential risks include:

- Malware
- Malicious applications
- Screen overlays
- Remote-access tools
- SIM-related attacks
- Phishing
- Social engineering
- Stolen credentials

Users should avoid installing untrusted applications or sharing
authentication information.

The chatbot should never ask users to disable security controls.

============================================================
32. PHISHING
============================================================

Phishing is a social-engineering technique in which an attacker
attempts to trick a user into revealing information or performing
an action.

Examples include:

- Fake payment links
- Fake customer-support messages
- Fake KYC messages
- Fake refunds
- Fake prize notifications
- Fake banking pages

A legitimate payment explanation bot should never request a
user's:

- UPI PIN
- OTP
- Password
- CVV
- ATM PIN

============================================================
33. QR CODE FRAUD
============================================================

Fraudsters can use QR codes as part of social-engineering
attacks.

Important principle:

A QR code can be used to initiate a payment.

Scanning a QR code does not inherently mean that money will be
received.

Before authenticating, a user should verify the transaction
details displayed by the payment application.

============================================================
34. SOCIAL ENGINEERING
============================================================

Social engineering manipulates people rather than directly
attacking technical systems.

Examples:

- Impersonating bank employees
- Impersonating customer support
- Fake KYC requests
- Fake refund requests
- Fake police/government messages
- Fake investment opportunities
- Urgent requests for OTPs or PINs

A security-aware payment system should combine technical
controls with customer awareness.

============================================================
35. FRAUD PREVENTION
============================================================

Fraud prevention can involve multiple layers.

Possible layers include:

1. Customer authentication
2. Device security
3. Transaction monitoring
4. Risk scoring
5. Velocity checks
6. Account controls
7. Fraud detection
8. Customer alerts
9. Transaction limits
10. Incident response
11. Dispute management

No single control can eliminate all fraud.

============================================================
36. FRAUD VS PAYMENT FAILURE
============================================================

A failed payment is not automatically fraud.

Examples:

Technical failure:
The bank or payment network is temporarily unavailable.

Business decline:
A transaction violates an applicable transaction condition.

Fraud:
A transaction may have been initiated through unauthorized or
deceptive activity.

These are different concepts.

============================================================
37. DISPUTE MANAGEMENT
============================================================

A payment dispute occurs when a customer, merchant or financial
institution questions a transaction or its outcome.

Examples:

- Payment debited but recipient not credited
- Incorrect transaction
- Duplicate transaction
- Unauthorized transaction
- Merchant dispute
- Failed transaction with unexpected account status

A dispute generally requires examination of transaction records
and applicable rules.

The chatbot should explain the conceptual process rather than
claiming to resolve a real dispute.

============================================================
38. GRIEVANCE REDRESSAL
============================================================

Customers can have complaints regarding digital-payment services.

A typical escalation concept is:

1. Contact the relevant payment service provider or bank.
2. Provide transaction/reference information.
3. Allow the entity to investigate.
4. Escalate through the applicable grievance mechanism if the
   issue is not resolved.

The Reserve Bank's Integrated Ombudsman framework provides a
grievance-redress mechanism for covered regulated entities.

The Reserve Bank - Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026 came into
force on July 1, 2026 and replaced the 2021 scheme.

The bot should not guarantee that a complaint will be accepted or
that compensation will be awarded.

============================================================
39. TRANSACTION RECONCILIATION
============================================================

Reconciliation is essential because a digital payment can involve
multiple systems.

Example:

Customer application
        ↓
PSP system
        ↓
NPCI/payment network
        ↓
Remitter bank
        ↓
Beneficiary bank
        ↓
Merchant system

Each system can maintain records.

Reconciliation checks whether those records agree.

============================================================
40. EXCEPTIONS
============================================================

An exception is a transaction condition that requires special
handling.

Examples:

- Timeout
- Duplicate request
- Delayed response
- Missing transaction
- Amount mismatch
- Incorrect status
- Failed credit
- Failed debit
- Reversal
- Dispute

Exception handling can involve:

- Investigation
- Reconciliation
- Status verification
- Adjustment
- Reversal
- Customer communication
- Escalation

The exact procedure depends on the payment system and rules.

============================================================
41. TRANSACTION TIMEOUT
============================================================

A timeout occurs when a system does not receive a required
response within the expected period.

A timeout does not automatically mean that the transaction failed.

The system may need to determine the final transaction status
before deciding the appropriate next step.

This is why users should avoid immediately repeating a payment
without checking the transaction status.

Repeated payment attempts can potentially result in duplicate
transactions when the original transaction was actually
successful but its response was delayed.

============================================================
42. REVERSAL
============================================================

A reversal is a transaction adjustment that returns or corrects
funds after a transaction outcome requires reversal.

A reversal is different from a new independent payment.

The exact reversal process depends on:

- Payment system
- Transaction state
- Bank processing
- Applicable rules
- Reason for reversal

============================================================
43. PAYMENT CONFIRMATION
============================================================

A payment confirmation is a status indication from a payment
system.

A screenshot or message saying "payment successful" should not be
treated as an independent source of truth for every business
process.

Merchants should reconcile payment status with their own
transaction records.

Customers should preserve transaction/reference information when
raising disputes.

============================================================
44. DATA PROTECTION AND PRIVACY
============================================================

Digital payment systems process sensitive information.

Examples can include:

- Account identifiers
- Transaction information
- Device information
- Authentication information
- Customer identity information
- Contact information

A secure payment architecture should minimize unnecessary access
to sensitive information.

The FinTech Explainer Bot itself must not request or store
sensitive payment credentials.

============================================================
45. PRINCIPLE OF LEAST PRIVILEGE
============================================================

Least privilege means a system or user should receive only the
access necessary to perform its required function.

For a payment application this can mean:

- Limited API permissions
- Limited database access
- Separation of duties
- Restricted administrative access
- Strong authentication
- Logging and monitoring

A chatbot should operate with no access to real banking accounts
or transaction execution systems.

============================================================
46. API SECURITY
============================================================

APIs allow software systems to communicate.

Payment APIs should be protected against:

- Unauthorized access
- Credential theft
- Replay attacks
- Injection
- Abuse
- Excessive requests
- Data leakage

Important API security concepts include:

- Authentication
- Authorization
- Encryption
- Rate limiting
- Input validation
- Logging
- Monitoring
- Secret management

API keys should never be hard-coded into public source code.

============================================================
47. SECRET MANAGEMENT
============================================================

API keys, passwords and other secrets must not be placed directly
inside source code.

Bad practice:

api_key = "actual-secret-key"

Better practice:

Load secrets through environment variables or an appropriate
secret-management system.

Example:

GEMINI_API_KEY=...

The .env file should normally be excluded from Git using:

.env

The FinTech bot should not expose secrets through its UI.

============================================================
48. AUDIT LOGGING
============================================================

Audit logs help organizations understand what happened in a
system.

Useful information can include:

- Event timestamp
- System component
- Transaction/reference ID
- Event type
- Status
- Error category
- Administrative action

Logs should avoid exposing unnecessary sensitive information.

============================================================
49. TRANSACTION REFERENCE ID
============================================================

A transaction reference ID is an identifier associated with a
transaction.

Reference IDs help systems and support teams:

- Find transactions
- Investigate failures
- Reconcile records
- Handle disputes
- Trace transaction states

The exact identifier format depends on the payment system.

============================================================
50. PAYMENT GATEWAY VS PAYMENT NETWORK
============================================================

These concepts should not be confused.

Payment gateway:
A technology/service layer that can facilitate payment
communication between a merchant and payment ecosystem.

Payment network:
Infrastructure and rules through which payment transactions are
routed and processed.

UPI is a payment system/network infrastructure, not simply a
merchant website payment button.

============================================================
51. PSP VS BANK
============================================================

A PSP provides payment-service functionality.

A bank can participate in UPI in multiple roles, including
holding customer accounts and performing remitter or beneficiary
functions.

The same organization may participate in different capacities
depending on the applicable UPI arrangement.

============================================================
52. NPCI VS RBI
============================================================

RBI and NPCI have different roles.

RBI:
- Central bank of India
- Regulator/overseer for relevant payment systems and regulated
  entities
- Issues regulatory directions in applicable areas
- Oversees payment-system and banking-related frameworks

NPCI:
- Operates/co-ordinates UPI infrastructure and related payment
  systems
- Provides network-level services
- Coordinates participating members
- Performs applicable clearing and settlement functions

A chatbot should never describe NPCI as if it were the same thing
as RBI.

============================================================
53. COMPLIANCE CHECKS
============================================================

"Compliance checks" is a broad term.

Depending on the context, compliance checks can involve:

- KYC requirements
- Customer due diligence
- AML controls
- CFT controls
- Sanctions screening
- Transaction monitoring
- Risk management
- Regulatory reporting
- Record keeping
- Security controls
- Internal policies
- Applicable transaction rules

Not every transaction necessarily triggers every possible
compliance check.

Controls depend on:

- Customer
- Institution
- Product
- Transaction
- Risk level
- Applicable law
- Applicable regulatory directions

============================================================
54. COMPLIANCE CHECK VS FRAUD CHECK
============================================================

Compliance and fraud controls overlap but are not identical.

Compliance checks:
Focus on regulatory and legal obligations.

Fraud checks:
Focus on detecting and preventing fraudulent or unauthorized
activity.

Example:

A transaction may be legitimate but require compliance review.

Another transaction may violate fraud-risk rules without being a
traditional AML issue.

A payment system can use multiple control layers simultaneously.

============================================================
55. COMPLIANCE CHECK VS AUTHENTICATION
============================================================

Authentication:
"Is the required identity/credential verified?"

Compliance:
"Does the activity satisfy applicable regulatory and institutional
requirements?"

Fraud detection:
"Does the activity appear suspicious, unauthorized or risky?"

These are different concepts.

============================================================
56. TRANSACTION RISK
============================================================

Transaction risk can be influenced by factors such as:

- Amount
- Frequency
- Customer behavior
- Device behavior
- Location-related signals
- Beneficiary characteristics
- Historical patterns
- Known fraud indicators
- Applicable regulatory requirements

A risk engine may combine multiple signals.

The exact scoring methodology is normally proprietary to the
financial institution or payment provider.

============================================================
57. MACHINE LEARNING IN FRAUD DETECTION
============================================================

Financial institutions may use automated systems and machine
learning to identify potentially suspicious transactions.

Possible input features can include:

- Transaction amount
- Transaction frequency
- Time patterns
- Device information
- Historical behavior
- Beneficiary patterns
- Network relationships
- Previous fraud indicators

A model can produce a risk score.

Example:

Transaction
    ↓
Feature extraction
    ↓
Risk model
    ↓
Risk score
    ↓
Low-risk / Review / Block
    ↓
Additional action

The exact thresholds and decisions depend on the institution.

Machine-learning predictions should not automatically be treated
as proof of fraud.

============================================================
58. FALSE POSITIVE
============================================================

A false positive occurs when a legitimate activity is incorrectly
identified as suspicious or risky.

Example:

A customer makes an unusually large but legitimate payment and a
fraud system flags it for review.

False positives are important because excessive blocking can
harm legitimate customers.

============================================================
59. FALSE NEGATIVE
============================================================

A false negative occurs when suspicious or fraudulent activity is
not detected.

Fraud systems must therefore balance:

- Detection
- Customer experience
- False positives
- False negatives
- Operational cost
- Regulatory requirements

============================================================
60. TRANSACTION LIMITS
============================================================

Payment systems can impose transaction limits.

Limits can depend on:

- Payment type
- Account type
- Bank
- Product
- Risk controls
- Regulatory requirements
- Applicable NPCI rules
- Merchant category
- Customer configuration

IMPORTANT:

The bot should NOT hard-code a supposedly permanent UPI limit
unless the source and date are known.

Limits can change.

If a user asks:

"What is the current UPI limit?"

the ideal system should retrieve the latest authoritative
information rather than rely on an old static answer.

65. EXAMPLE — EXPLAINING A SIMPLE UPI PAYMENT
============================================================

Question:

"Explain a simple UPI payment."

Recommended conceptual answer:

1. The payer chooses a recipient and starts a payment.
2. The payment details are entered or obtained through a QR code,
   UPI ID or another supported mechanism.
3. The payment application displays the transaction details.
4. The payer verifies the recipient and amount.
5. The payer completes the required authentication.
6. The transaction is processed through the UPI ecosystem.
7. The payer's account is debited and the beneficiary's account is
   credited when the transaction completes successfully.
8. The payment system provides a transaction status.
9. Participating institutions perform applicable clearing,
   settlement and reconciliation processes.

============================================================
66. EXAMPLE — EXPLAINING AUTHENTICATION
============================================================

Question:

"What is authentication?"

Answer concept:

Authentication is the process of verifying that the required user
or credential satisfies the authentication mechanism.

In a UPI context, the UPI PIN is an important authentication
mechanism for applicable transactions.

The PIN should remain secret.

The FinTech bot should explain authentication but never request
the user's actual authentication credentials.

============================================================
67. EXAMPLE — EXPLAINING SETTLEMENT
============================================================

Question:

"What is settlement?"

Answer concept:

Settlement is the process through which participating financial
institutions discharge the financial obligations created by
payment transactions.

For UPI, NPCI performs applicable clearing and settlement
functions between participating members.

A customer-facing payment confirmation and inter-bank settlement
are related but technically distinct concepts.

============================================================
68. EXAMPLE — EXPLAINING KYC
============================================================

Question:

"What is KYC?"

Answer concept:

KYC means Know Your Customer.

It is a framework used by regulated entities to identify and
understand customers and manage financial-crime and other
regulatory risks.

KYC-related processes can include customer identification,
customer due diligence, beneficial-owner identification and
ongoing due diligence as applicable.

============================================================
69. EXAMPLE — EXPLAINING AML
============================================================

Question:

"What is AML?"

Answer concept:

AML means Anti-Money Laundering.

AML controls are designed to help prevent and detect the use of
financial systems for money laundering and related financial
crime.

AML controls can include:

- Customer due diligence
- Transaction monitoring
- Risk assessment
- Record keeping
- Investigation
- Reporting obligations

============================================================
70. EXAMPLE — EXPLAINING A FAILED PAYMENT
============================================================

Question:

"Why did my UPI payment fail?"

The bot should NOT guess the exact reason.

It should explain possible categories:

- Technical failure
- Network issue
- Bank availability issue
- Invalid transaction information
- Authentication failure
- Transaction limit
- Risk controls
- Insufficient funds
- Beneficiary-related issue
- Other bank or payment-system rules

The bot should advise the user to check the transaction status
and contact the relevant bank/payment provider for the actual
reason.

============================================================
71. EXAMPLE — PAYMENT DEBITED BUT RECIPIENT NOT CREDITED
============================================================

Question:

"Money was debited but the receiver didn't get it. What happened?"

The bot should explain:

This can occur when the transaction status between different
systems is delayed or when an exception occurs.

Possible stages include:

Payer account
    ↓
Payment network
    ↓
Beneficiary bank
    ↓
Recipient account

If the final state is not immediately available, the transaction
may require status confirmation, reconciliation or reversal
according to the applicable process.

The bot should NOT claim that the money is definitely lost or
definitely refunded without transaction-specific evidence.

78. IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS
============================================================

The bot should maintain these distinctions:

Authentication
=
Verifying the required identity or credential.

Authorization
=
Allowing or rejecting a transaction.

Validation
=
Checking whether transaction information satisfies required
conditions.

Processing
=
Technical handling of the transaction.

Clearing
=
Determining obligations between participating institutions.

Settlement
=
Discharging financial obligations between participating
institutions.

Reconciliation
=
Comparing records to identify mismatches or exceptions.

Compliance
=
Meeting applicable legal, regulatory and institutional
requirements.

Fraud detection
=
Identifying potentially fraudulent or unauthorized activity.
